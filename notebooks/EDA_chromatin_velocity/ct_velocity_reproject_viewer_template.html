Chromatin velocity on the celltype × stage peak atlas

One point = one genomic peak. The fixed atlas summarizes accessibility over the developmental course. Arrows project a peak’s fitted accessibility changes toward neighboring peak profiles; they do not track physical movement or conversion of one peak into another.

Black arrows show projected directions. Arrow length is a display choice, not opening/closing speed. No root or global direction is imposed.

Net rate averages valid fitted changes across the 21 consistently sampled celltypes. Opposing changes across celltypes can cancel. A peak with little net change may still have a substantial multidimensional rate.

Displayed arrows pass analysis filters for temporal change and projection coherence. These filters are conditional diagnostics; they are not biological confidence probabilities.

Methods, sampling, and interpretation

The celltype × stage atlas uses log1p(CPM), a globally centered PCA, and one fixed 2D or 3D UMAP. Models were trained on 20,000 peaks; all 640,830 peaks were mapped through those frozen models. The background shown here is the same 8,038-peak subset used by the earlier atlas explorer. Search is limited to that subset. Arrow origins are sampled separately from eligible peaks across the full atlas, with spatially balanced random sampling; changing the density shows nested subsets.

The same precomputed six-stage accessibility fits and interval rates are reused. Intervals use their actual durations: 2, 2, 2, 3, and 5 hours. The updated projection finds neighbors in the whole-course log-accessibility PCA and recalculates transition weights using the current interval’s linear-CPM state and rate vectors across 21 consistently sampled celltypes. The control retains the previous transition weights and changes only the destination coordinates. Neither mode refits the biological accessibility curves.

Arrow vectors stored in the payload are probability-weighted mean unit directions to neighboring peak coordinates. Their length reflects projection coherence, not a biological velocity in UMAP units/hour. Equal-length display normalizes each nonzero vector before applying a common axis-span scale; the weighted option preserves relative vector lengths. Only the display multiplier changes when the scale slider moves.

Colors use the original linear CPM/hour rates even though the atlas geometry uses log accessibility. Net-rate colors share a symmetric range across all intervals and both methods, clipped at the 98th percentile of absolute rates among all supported-changing peaks across the five intervals, matching the static figures. RMS-rate colors use the corresponding 98th percentile of full-atlas magnitudes. Both rate and magnitude colors apply the same supported-change source gate as the static figures; other peaks are gray. Hover labels and the selected-peak chart still expose the fitted summaries, including values whose arrows are withheld. Relative CPM cannot establish absolute genome-wide opening. Independent-cell tests found modest signal concentrated among stronger changes; a global root-to-branch stream has not been established. Nearest genes are annotations, not validated regulatory assignments.
