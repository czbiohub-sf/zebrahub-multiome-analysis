# Real peak-atlas specialization: methods and exploratory results

Updated 2026-10-20. This analysis transfers the specificity and guided-arrow ideas
from [the synthetic benchmark](SPECIALIZATION_FLOW.md) to the existing zebrafish
peak atlas. Each point is one fixed genomic peak. The primary scalar quantities
are accessibility changes within a celltype and changes in celltype specificity.
A displayed arrow maps those quantities onto supported relationships among peak
profiles; it does not establish genomic transport or peak ancestry.

**Treat original-depth and depth-matched fields as paired sensitivity views.**
A constant-accessibility simulation at the actual library depths can reproduce a
strong late specificity increase. This establishes sensitivity to observation
depth; it does not establish how much of the real trend is technical or exclude
biological change. Matching preserves each observed relative CPM in expectation
before filtering, but nonlinear specificity scores, reduced counts and support
filters can obscure genuine signals. The matched prototype is a controlled
comparison, not a correction to known biological truth. See
[the depth diagnostic](#depth-only-diagnostic) and
[the direct comparison](outputs/real_specialization_depth_matched_v1/07_original_vs_depth_matched_entropy.png).
Direct CPM changes and celltype-specific curves remain separate quantities.

## Normalization and zero semantics

The upstream [pseudobulk utility](../../scripts/utils/pseudobulk_utils.py) pools
raw counts by group, then scales every group to one common coverage:

```text
normalized[i,g] = raw_sum[i,g] * common_scale_factor / total_coverage[g]
CPM[i,g] = normalized[i,g] * 1,000,000 / common_scale_factor
```

The celltype object's stored common scale is **7,029,805.5**. The primary pipeline
uses CPM, preserving a transparent normalization unit across stages. These
values are relative library-normalized counts. They are not bounded
accessibility probabilities or fractions of cells with an open element.

The raw summed-count sample is integer-valued, and the normalized and log layers
match the formulas. The prior full scan found no nonfinite entries in `.X` and
27,893,354 zero entries. A zero in a **present** group represents an observed zero
aggregated count; it is not evidence that an absent group existed and was closed.

The two missing celltype-stage combinations are absent columns:

- `hemangioblasts`, 10 hpf.
- `notochord`, 24 hpf.

Populate a celltype-stage grid with a separate observation mask. Never fill these
missing conditions, or excluded low-cell groups, with observed zeros when
calculating specificity or temporal rates. Likewise, the toy model's 0.02 signal
floor and additive pseudocounts cannot be transferred without stating their units
and checking sensitivity on the real CPM distribution.

## Primary comparison set: fixed 21 celltypes

The primary specificity calculation uses the **21 celltypes with at least 20
cells at all six stages**, while retaining the complete 190-column state matrix
and metadata for celltype-level exploration. A fixed category set prevents changes
in celltype availability from changing the meaning of the stage-wise score.
The audit records the exact retained names.

| Minimum cells per group | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 19 | 24 hpf | Complete celltypes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| No additional threshold | 31 | 32 | 32 | 32 | 32 | 31 | 30 |
| ≥20 | 28 | 31 | 30 | 31 | 31 | 25 | 21 |
| ≥50 | 27 | 29 | 30 | 30 | 30 | 20 | 16 |

At the 20-cell threshold, adjacent-stage intersections contain
`[28, 30, 30, 31, 25]` celltypes. These paired-support rates provide a useful
sensitivity analysis, but their category sets differ across intervals. Compare
them as explicitly labeled alternatives to the primary fixed-21 analysis.
All six primordial-germ-cell groups have fewer than 20 cells, so they are excluded
by that rule. Groups such as late NMPs, epidermis, muscle, and tail bud also lack
adequate support at 24 hpf.

For a supported celltype, opening/closing is
`(CPM[t+1] - CPM[t]) / delta_hpf`, using gaps `[2,2,2,3,5]`. Both groups require at
least 20 cells and their combined raw count for that peak must be at least 20.
This permits supported zero-to-opening and closing-to-zero changes. In contrast,
specificity rates require at least 20 total raw counts across the score panel at
each endpoint, since an all-zero endpoint has undefined specificity. For a changing category
set, recalculate both endpoint specificity scores on the common observed
celltypes; subtracting scores computed on different sets can fabricate a change.

## Depth-matched specificity estimator

The full-atlas matched-depth calculation is implemented by
[depth_matched_real_benchmark.py](depth_matched_real_benchmark.py) and
[depth_matched_specialization.py](../../scripts/chrom_velo/depth_matched_specialization.py).
It retains the fixed 21-celltype panel, original peak identities, atlas graph and
2D/3D coordinates. Only specificity estimates and their derived structural and
guided fields change. The measured `X` matrix, celltype-level accessibility curves,
`accessibility_rate` layer and net/RMS accessibility summaries remain at their
original CPM values.

For celltype `c`, let `L[c,t]` be its original total coverage and
`D[c] = min_t L[c,t]`. For each of five independent draws, thin the observed count
`K[i,c,t]` with probability `p[c,t] = D[c] / L[c,t]`, then normalize by `D[c]`:

```text
K_thinned[r,i,c,t] ~ Binomial(K[i,c,t], p[c,t])
CPM_thinned[r,i,c,t] = K_thinned[r,i,c,t] * 1,000,000 / D[c]
S_mean[i,t] = mean_r(specificity(CPM_thinned[r,i,:,t]))
rate[i,t] = (S_mean[i,t+1] - S_mean[i,t]) / delta_hpf
```

A peak-stage score is retained **only if all five draws have positive signal and
at least 20 pooled counts across the fixed panel**. Both endpoint scores must pass
to retain a rate. The seed is 4242, and independent random streams use
`SeedSequence([4242, global_start, replicate_index])` with **fixed 512-row blocks**;
changing the block partition changes the draws. The analysis averages the five
scores, rather than computing one score from averaged counts. These differ because
specificity is nonlinear. No pseudocount or shrinkage is introduced.

The H5AD retains the original quantities as
`obsm['tau_uncorrected_specificity']`, `obsm['tau_uncorrected_rate']` and matching
entropy keys. The usual `tau_specificity`/`tau_rate` and entropy keys hold the
matched estimates. `depth_matched_mean_raw_total` and
`depth_matched_min_raw_total` record the mean/minimum pooled count across draws at
each stage. The original `fixed_raw_total` still describes measured counts.
Inherited `available_*`, `min_cells50_*` and `shrinkage05_*` sensitivity arrays
belong to the original-depth comparison; they are explicitly marked as such in
`uns['real_specialization']` and are not matched-depth sensitivity analyses.

### What matching controls, and what remains uncertain

Within-celltype matching makes each celltype's exposure constant through time
under the constant-CPM null. It does **not** equalize exposure between celltypes:
targets range from **274,216 counts for PSM to 7,177,892 for neural_floor_plate**, a
26.18-fold difference. Matched target coverage totals 52,097,689 counts per stage,
versus original totals of 222,737,042; 278,582,761; 174,923,118; 285,471,398;
359,076,846; and 59,626,993. Unequal cross-celltype noise can still affect the
maximum-based tau score and entropy. Matching is a sensitivity control, not an
unbiased estimate of latent specificity or proof of biological change.

Nineteen of 21 celltypes have their lowest coverage at 24 hpf; the exceptions,
hematopoietic_vasculature and differentiating_neurons, have minima at 10 hpf. At a
minimum-depth stage, `p=1`, so repeated thinning adds no variation even though the
original measurement remains uncertain. `tau_thinning_sign_agreement` and the
entropy equivalent record the fraction of five draw-specific rates agreeing with
the mean rate's sign. These are **conditional Monte Carlo stability** scores.
All draws share the same original measured counts; they are not biological
replicates, confidence intervals, or an estimate of total measurement uncertainty.
The default matched temporal-arrow display additionally requires at least **four
of five signs to agree** (`thinning_sign_agreement >= 0.8`). Mean scalar rates and
structural directions remain unchanged by this display mask. This filter was
added because only about half of effect-eligible early/middle tau rates, and
57–62% of corresponding entropy rates, passed that conditional-sign criterion.

Arrow lengths in static figures and the browser are display choices for
readability; static 2D arrows use a longer scale than the browser default.
Inspect scalar specificity or CPM/hpf values for change magnitude. A drawn arrow
length is neither measured accessibility speed nor movement of a genomic peak.

Requiring all five draws to pass count support is conservative and dependent on
the number of draws: a borderline peak-stage with a 50% single-draw pass
probability passes all five with probability 3.125%. Report support loss as part
of the result. Future sensitivity checks could separate an expected-count support
criterion from the fraction of draws passing, rather than making support depend
implicitly on the number of simulations.

CPM remains compositional, and coarse-celltype composition and stage-specific
library effects remain possible explanations for observed differences. Equalizing
all 21 celltypes to the global minimum would retain only 5,758,536 counts per
stage—another 89% loss relative to within-celltype matching—so that stricter
control should be assessed as a separate sensitivity analysis. A fitted count
model could account for unequal precision without discarding as much depth, but
would need checked assumptions and would not create missing biological replication.

## Original-depth count-support selection and tau plateaus

An [independent full raw-count scan](outputs/real_specialization_v1/count_support_plateau_audit.json)
quantifies the primary evidence gate on the fixed panel:

| Stage, hpf | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 19 | 24 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Peaks with ≥20 counts | 615,815 | 632,073 | 598,405 | 632,866 | 634,360 | 428,826 |
| Peaks with ≥50 counts | 481,605 | 556,315 | 434,520 | 546,828 | 572,013 | 180,311 |

The original-depth terminal score therefore covers **428,826 peaks (66.92%)**, excluding
212,004 peaks at 24 hpf. Requiring 50 counts would retain only 180,311 (28.14%).
426,823 peaks pass the 20-count gate at all six stages. The original-depth terminal structural
field and late-interval rates consequently represent a count-selected subset;
less visible flow at the last stage cannot be read directly as less biological
change.

Among terminal peaks passing the 20-count gate, **none have tau exactly 1**, and
none are detected in only one of the 21 celltypes. Only 22 have tau at least 0.99;
7,030 have tau at least 0.95. The maximum is 0.99571. Exact single-celltype tau
plateaus therefore do not explain unsupported outward arrows in this dataset.
A high-scoring peak can still lack sufficiently higher-scoring neighbors, which
is a structural endpoint rather than evidence of zero temporal change. Separate
downhill fields preserve supported decreasing-specificity directions there.

## Specificity and arrow interpretation

Both primary real-data fields use **24 hpf as their structural reference stage**
and the same fixed 21-celltype panel. This isolates the specificity-definition
comparison from changes in temporal weighting:

- **Tau at 24 hpf:** restriction relative to the most accessible celltype at the
  final stage. This matches the proposed map of late regulatory specialization.
- **Entropy specificity at 24 hpf:** concentration of total accessibility mass
  across those same celltypes at that same stage.

For a stage with `C` observed celltypes and nonnegative CPM `x_c`, the two
scale-invariant definitions are:

```text
tau = sum_c(1 - x_c / max_c(x_c)) / (C - 1)
p_c = x_c / sum_c(x_c)
entropy_specificity = 1 + sum_c(p_c * log(p_c)) / log(C)
```

Zero-mass entropy terms contribute zero. A whole-stage zero vector has undefined
specificity. Both scores range from broad/uniform (0) to maximally restricted (1),
but tau is relative to the largest value while entropy considers all mass.

This differs deliberately from the synthetic pair (terminal tau versus
duration-averaged entropy). Duration-averaged scores are retained as sensitivity
diagnostics rather than as the primary real entropy orientation.

The synthetic audit showed that these are different biological questions, not
interchangeable estimates of one latent order. Background accessibility and
activation timing can reverse their ranking of programs. The real-data analysis
should retain both comparisons and avoid orienting arrows solely to look
radially outward from a chosen root.

Structural arrows follow existing atlas graph edges toward the chosen terminal
specificity score. The matched score averages five specificity estimates from nonnegative thinned
CPM, with no pseudocount or shrinkage; every draw must meet the 20-count gate. The
original-depth comparison calculates specificity directly from measured CPM and
applies the gate once. Both endpoints must be supported to compute a rate. The stage-wise panel has at least two
observed celltypes. Unsupported scores and rates remain NaN.

For an existing graph edge, the primary weight is proportional to
`graph_weight × max(S_neighbor - S_source - 0.01, 0)`. Weights are normalized within
each source peak. Projected vectors average unit directions to the supported
neighbors in the existing UMAP. A separate decreasing-score field supplies
downhill directions; it is not obtained by drawing an unsupported negative
extrapolation at a tip. Temporal guided arrows use specificity change to select
supported uphill or downhill directions. Opening/closing colors are separate:
a peak may become more specific through closing outside its target lineage.
Broad-accessibility reference peaks are selected from peaks with valid scores
at all stages: the bottom 5% of maximum stage matched tau, original minimum-stage mean CPM above
the cohort median, and detectable accessibility in at least 80% of fixed-panel
celltypes at every stage. This is an explicit descriptive reference criterion.
It does not require exactly constant accessibility and does not prove that a
peak is accessible in every individual cell. Its location establishes neither
a developmental ancestor nor a privileged origin of the directed graph.

## Net accessibility change and change magnitude

The full result also separates opening/closing magnitude from the guided arrows.
For each interval, calculate CPM/hpf changes across the fixed 21 celltypes:

```text
mean_rate[i,t] = mean_celltypes(accessibility_rate[i,celltype,t])
rms_rate[i,t] = sqrt(mean_celltypes(accessibility_rate[i,celltype,t]^2))
```

The two interval endpoints together require at least 20 pooled raw counts over
that fixed panel. This gate differs from specificity, which needs signal at both
endpoints. Opposing celltype changes can cancel in the signed mean; RMS preserves
their magnitude. Neither quantity measures speed through the UMAP.

The whole-time signed summary is `sum(delta_hpf * mean_rate) / 14`. The magnitude
summary is `sum(delta_hpf * rms_rate) / 14`, a **duration-weighted mean of the
interval RMS values**. It is not a single RMS taken over all celltype-time entries.
Both whole-time summaries require all five intervals to be supported.

The output H5AD stores interval values in
`obsm['fixed_accessibility_mean_rate']` and
`obsm['fixed_accessibility_rms_rate']`, and whole-time values in
`obs['net_accessibility_rate']` and `obs['rms_accessibility_rate']`.
`cluster_accessibility_summary.csv` reports per-cluster interval means for both.
The figure `06_real_accessibility_magnitude_and_net_change.png` displays signed
net change beside magnitude, with a PDF version for export.

| --- | --- | ---: |
| 10 hpf | TDR126 | 14,970 |
| 12 hpf | TDR127 | 18,963 |

`orig.ident` is `SeuratProject` for every cell and cannot identify replicates.

Real-data fields therefore require sensitivity and support diagnostics rather
than importing simulated replicate SEs or claiming biological confidence
intervals. Cell resampling could measure within-library sampling variability,
but cannot separate developmental changes from stage-specific library effects.
The prototype labels arrow filters as support/stability criteria, not
statistically validated velocity confidence. The display requires at least two
outgoing neighbors, directional coherence at least 0.25, and mean directional
cosine agreement of at least 0.8 across three parameter alternatives:

- Edge tolerance 0.005, gradient exponent 1.
- Edge tolerance 0.02, gradient exponent 1.
- Edge tolerance 0.01, gradient exponent 2.

The primary setting is tolerance 0.01 and exponent 1. An absent alternative
direction contributes zero agreement, rather than being silently omitted. These
are closely related parameter perturbations on one fixed graph and embedding;
their agreement is a conditional sensitivity diagnostic.

Guided temporal arrows additionally require an absolute specificity rate above
`0.002 / hpf`. This effect-size threshold is a display choice, not a significance
threshold. Parameter stability does not include cell resampling or uncertainty in
the biological annotations. Rate sensitivity tables separately compare 5%
shrinkage, the fixed 16-celltype panel obtained with at least 50 cells per group,
and the available-celltype panel with matched interval endpoints.

## What was actually tuned or swept

**The real-data fields were sensitivity-checked, not globally optimized.** There
is no real-data ground-truth velocity or held-out biological objective to optimize.
The existing master graph and both UMAP layouts remain fixed.

### Executed real-data comparisons

Both [original-depth](outputs/real_specialization_v1/parameter_sensitivity.csv)
and [matched-depth](outputs/real_specialization_depth_matched_v1/parameter_sensitivity.csv)
runs evaluated these four `(edge tolerance, gradient exponent)` settings:

| Role | Tolerance | Exponent |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Reference field | 0.01 | 1 |
| Lower edge threshold | 0.005 | 1 |
| Higher edge threshold | 0.02 | 1 |
| Stronger weighting of score differences | 0.01 | 2 |

Across the 24 matched comparisons (three alternatives × two definitions × two
directions × two embeddings), median direction cosines range from **0.99209 to
0.99993**; **95.54–99.85%** of compared directions have cosine at least 0.8. The
original-depth ranges are 0.99191–0.99993 and 95.45–99.86%. These are conditional
comparisons: the reference must have coherence ≥0.25 and both settings must have a
finite direction. They are not percentages of all peaks, biological accuracy or
coverage. The actual display-stability calculation is stricter: an absent
alternative direction contributes zero agreement.

Additional original-depth score checks compared shrinkage 0 versus 0.05, fixed
21 versus fixed 16 celltypes (minimum cell counts 20 versus 50), and paired
available-celltype panels. The panel comparison changes the biological comparison
set as well as support. Terminal versus duration-mean score ranks were compared,
but neither stage weights nor a real-data loss function were fitted. Both real
structural fields deliberately use **24 hpf**, while interval rates use the actual
2/2/2/3/5-hour gaps. The matched inherited alternative-panel arrays remain
original-depth diagnostics, not new matched-panel sweeps.

These were **fixed or pragmatic filters**, without a systematic real-data grid
search: minimum two outgoing neighbors, coherence 0.25, average parameter cosine
0.8, rate effect floor 0.002/hpf, and four-of-five thinning-sign agreement. Matching
uses five draws, seed 4242, per-celltype minimum coverage and the all-five ≥20-count
gate; the number of draws, target rule and support rule were not optimized. There
was no real-data neighbor-count sweep, UMAP retuning, Cicero-threshold/mixture
sweep, or search for a more outward-looking field.

### Earlier synthetic calibration

The [synthetic benchmark](SPECIALIZATION_FLOW.md#calibration-and-executed-results)
used known-truth objectives on seeds 42/43, followed by development inspection on
44 and final computational evaluation on 45. Its executed candidate grids were:

| Component | Candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Score estimator | Pseudocount `{0, .001, .01}`; shrinkage `{0, .05}`; jackknife bias correction off/on |
| Structural graph | Neighbors `{15,30,60}`; stage weighting `{equal,duration,late,terminal}`; tolerance `{0,.0001,.0005,.001}`; exponent `{.5,1,2}` |
| Temporal-rate filter | SE multiplier `{2.57,3,4}`; absolute floor `{0,.0001,.0003,.001}` |
| Edge-noise margin | SE multiplier `{0,.5,1,1.5,2}` |

Score selection minimized program-averaged rate RMSE. Edge selection rewarded
probability-weighted agreement with true specificity increases and penalized
missing hierarchy coverage; the later edge-margin step also penalized arrows in
uniform-opening controls. Rate filtering maximized recall subject to empirical
worst-null-program false positives ≤5% and accepted-rate sign accuracy ≥95%.
Stage weighting remained an explicitly chosen question: terminal tau versus
duration-mean entropy. It was not selected to make arrows appear outward. These
simulation-specific settings and SE filters were not transferred wholesale to
the real atlas, which has different scaling, an existing graph and no comparable
replicate design.

### Appearance was adjusted separately

Point/arrow sampling, opacity, shaft width and display length were adjusted for
legibility. Static 2D arrows have normalized length equal to 1.6% of the atlas
bounding-box diagonal; the browser and static 3D base length is 0.8%. Static plots
show up to 240 structural 2D arrows or 180 temporal/3D arrows, selected for spatial
coverage among already-eligible vectors. None of these choices changes saved
specificity, temporal rates or graph directions. A visually smooth or attractive
field is not an optimization metric or evidence of biological correctness.

`paths/` directories. These are display-fidelity diagnostics, not held-out
biological validation.

The [interpolation helper](../../scripts/chrom_velo/streamline_field.py) first
normalizes eligible source vectors to unit length, then averages local directions
with distance weights. Ineligible nearby peaks remain in the denominator when
checking how much of a neighborhood supports a field. Thus streamlines summarize
**direction**, while the background's scalar rate colors separately encode
change magnitude. Smoothing choices are not estimates of a biological spatial
scale; numerical defaults and alternatives are retained in the manifest rather
than inferred from an attractive rendering.

The final grid uses 180 nodes along its long axis, at most 50 nearest peaks,
Gaussian bandwidth 0.75 grid cells and a finite radius of 1.5 grid cells. A node
requires at least five eligible neighboring peaks, effective weighted support of
at least three, eligible weight fraction ≥0.2, mean-direction coherence ≥0.35 and
an eligible peak within 0.8 grid cells. The bandwidth comparison is `{0.5,0.75,1}`
with other grid settings fixed. Drawing uses density 3, minimum length 0.005,
maximum length 0.65 and deterministic seeds at supported interpolation-cell
centres. These are declared rendering choices, not fitted biological parameters.

The [path renderer](../../scripts/chrom_velo/streamline_paths.py) treats
Matplotlib's integrated paths as candidates. It inserts crossed grid boundaries,
checks support and forward alignment along short segments, and splits or removes
unsupported or locally reversed pieces. The default minimum tangent-to-grid
cosine is 0.1. Arrowheads have separate support checks. This guards against a
Matplotlib integration step extending past a masked boundary; it does not change
the source vectors, numerical grid or support mask. Per-panel pruning counts are
recorded in the manifest, separately from the source-to-grid metrics below.

The independent [drawn-path audit](outputs/real_specialization_streams_v1/verification/streamline_path_audit.json)
passed across **26 panels and 20 unique grids**: 84,170 sampled path points had
zero unsupported positions or reversed segments, and 1,739 arrowheads had zero
support or orientation violations. Source-vector/mask hashes matched the saved
H5AD fields, whose size and modification time were unchanged. These checks verify
the rendering against its grid; they do not validate a biological trajectory or
resolve the source-to-grid disagreements below.

### How much of the source field do streams summarize?

Grid diagnostics sample **50,000 eligible source peaks per field**. The coverage
column below is the fraction of that sample lying in fully supported interpolation
cells. Direction agreement is measured **only within that supported subset**;
cosine below zero means the local mean opposes the individual source vector.
Ranges span the indicated fields, not confidence intervals.

| Saved fields | Sample covered by supported cells | Median source-to-grid cosine | Source directions opposed by local mean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Matched structural, tau and entropy | 6.53–11.68% | 0.835–0.843 | 20.0–23.5% |
| Matched temporal, both definitions × five intervals | 0.18–4.95% | 0.643–0.875 | 17.9–26.5% |
| Original structural, entropy | 11.00% | 0.847 | 20.4% |
| Original temporal, entropy × five intervals | 1.95–23.03% | 0.696–0.851 | 20.6–31.0% |

High stability to graph-parameter changes does not imply that a smooth 2D field
faithfully represents every peak arrow. A local mean can oppose a minority of
heterogeneous directions without an implementation error. The appropriate reading
is **local mean direction** on the supported subset, not a reconstruction of
individual peak trajectories. Sparse or short curves are expected when neighboring
vectors disagree or valid grid cells do not form a continuous region; this is not
necessarily a rendering failure and should not be hidden by forcing connections.
The saved diagnostics measure source-to-grid agreement separately from checks on
the actual drawn paths.

A streamplot locally interpolates the existing 2D vector field and traces curves
through that interpolated field. Its curve parameter is a drawing coordinate,
**not developmental time**. Each temporal panel represents one fixed measured
interval; following its line does not advance through the six developmental
stages. Structural streamlines indicate the chosen terminal-specificity
orientation, not a measured developmental trajectory.

Smoothing and line density affect appearance. They can average opposing directions
or join profiles that only appear close after UMAP projection. Sparse or
unsupported regions therefore need masks, and disconnected peak islands should
not be bridged just to draw continuous curves. A broad reference cluster is not
an imposed streamline source. Streamplots add a summary visualization; they do
not provide a new velocity estimate, evidence of peak-to-peak transport, or a
solution to the depth and celltype-composition limitations above.

### Depth-only diagnostic

The [supplemental diagnostic script](real_depth_null.py) samples **8,192 atlas
peaks uniformly without replacement**, with seed 42, and runs five independent
simulations for each of three arms:

1. **Constant CPM, actual depth.** For each peak and celltype, estimate a constant
   count rate as summed raw counts over all available stages divided by summed
   condition coverage. Simulate independent Poisson counts using that rate and
   each original condition's coverage. The expected CPM and true specificity are
   constant through time; measurement depth changes.
2. **Constant CPM, matched depth.** Use the same constant rates, but assign each
   celltype its minimum observed coverage at every stage. Expected CPM remains
   unchanged while the stage-dependent exposure differences are removed.
3. **Observed counts, matched depth.** Independently binomial-thin each observed
   peak count with probability `minimum_celltype_coverage / condition_coverage`.
   Normalize the thinned counts by the reduced coverage. This preserves observed
   relative differences in expectation while making each celltype's exposure
   constant across stages; it discards information.

Every arm uses the same fixed 21-celltype panel, 20-count score gate and
0.002/hpf effect floor as the primary analysis. The
[summary JSON](outputs/real_specialization_v1/depth_null_summary.json),
[complete statistics](outputs/real_specialization_v1/depth_null_statistics.csv),
[sampled peak IDs](outputs/real_specialization_v1/depth_null_sample_peaks.csv), and
[diagnostic figure](outputs/real_specialization_v1/depth_null_diagnostic.png)
record the calculation. In the following **19→24 hpf** table, simulation entries
are means of five replicate summaries; each arm initially uses its own supported
peak subset.

| Data / simulation | Tau median rate / hpf | Tau positive fraction | Entropy median rate / hpf | Entropy positive fraction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Observed sampled peaks | +0.03016 | 88.61% | +0.02819 | 98.36% |
| Constant CPM, actual depth | +0.03077 | 89.89% | +0.02717 | 99.07% |
| Constant CPM, matched depth | +0.00013 | 50.15% | +0.00002 | 50.40% |
| Observed counts, matched depth | +0.00245 | 55.47% | +0.00157 | 56.47% |

Positive fractions use only rates passing the absolute effect floor. The raw
observed interval supports 5,425 sampled peaks, compared with an average 5,106.6
under the actual-depth null, 4,213.6 under the matched-depth null and 4,017.2 after
observed-count thinning. These support differences are recorded rather than
hidden. On peaks shared by each simulated replicate and the observed interval,
the actual-depth null still has 99.01% positive entropy rates, compared with
98.26% for the observed data on that shared support. The corresponding tau
fractions are 89.41% and 87.89%. Thus the finding is not solely a different set of
peaks passing the count threshold.

**Variable depth alone can produce the dominant global late-specificity pattern
without any true temporal change.** The matched-depth null is approximately
symmetric around zero, and thinning the observed data sharply reduces the late
positive skew. The strong raw global direction therefore cannot be presented as
evidence of developmental specialization. Local peak biology can coexist with
this confounding, but neither the raw arrows nor this diagnostic distinguish it
reliably from technical effects on their own.

This is an explanatory model check, not a calibrated null hypothesis test,
confidence interval, or corrected velocity estimate. Poisson counts omit
biological overdispersion and cell-level dependence. Constant rates are fitted
using all observed stages; simulations are not independent validation data.
Binomial thinning cannot remove other stage-specific technical effects and
reduces power. No simulated counts replace the primary H5AD or overlays.
